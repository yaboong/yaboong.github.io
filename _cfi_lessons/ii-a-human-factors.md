### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands how the senses and the brain can be fooled or impaired in flight (illusions, hypoxia, alcohol and drugs, other physiological factors).
- The student can use aeronautical decision-making (ADM) and risk-management tools to judge whether he or she is fit to fly and to manage risk in flight.

### 2. Content

- Three sensory systems: vestibular, visual, somatosensory
- Spatial disorientation: ICEFLAGS (vestibular vs visual illusions)
- Optical illusions on approach: upslope/downslope, narrow/wide runway, featureless terrain, rain, haze; lower-approach risk and prevention (instruments, VASI/PAPI, preflight study)
- Hypoxia: hypoxic, stagnant, histotoxic, hypemic; symptoms, time of useful consciousness, 14 CFR 91.211 and FAA oxygen recommendations
- Alcohol and drugs (14 CFR 91.17), carbon monoxide poisoning and the cabin heat system, hyperventilation
- Middle ear and sinus blockage, flying with a cold, over-the-counter medications, scuba diving and decompression sickness
- IMSAFE, ADM, the five hazardous attitudes and antidotes, PAVE, DECIDE, 3P, CARE, TEAM
- Single-pilot resource management (SRM), crew resource management (CRM), confirmation and expectation bias
- Distraction and task prioritization (aviate, navigate, communicate)

### 3. Schedule (about 60 min)


| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Introduction (optical illusion demo, motion sickness question) | 5 min |
| Sensory systems, spatial disorientation, optical illusions | 15 min |
| Hypoxia, alcohol and drugs, carbon monoxide, other physiological factors | 15 min |
| IMSAFE, ADM tools, SRM/CRM, bias, distraction | 15 min |
| Scenario practice (PAVE, DECIDE, 3P) and review | 10 min |


### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the slide deck (videos: motion sickness, CRM, rainy LPV approach)
- Whiteboard and markers
- PA-28-161 POH (cabin heat system)
- FAA Personal Minimums Checklist, FAA over-the-counter medications guide

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Open with the optical-illusion slides and ask the student to judge before revealing the answer.
- Explain the three sensory systems and walk through ICEFLAGS with the diagrams.
- Compare runway-slope and runway-width illusions and ask which approach error is more dangerous.
- Explain the four types of hypoxia, then link symptoms, time of useful consciousness and the oxygen rules.
- Cover alcohol and drug rules, carbon monoxide, hyperventilation, ear and sinus problems, and medications.
- Teach IMSAFE, hazardous attitudes and the ADM mnemonics, then run the PAVE, DECIDE and 3P scenarios with the student.
- Ask review questions throughout and correct misunderstandings.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Answer the illusion and scenario questions before the explanation.
- Name and explain each ICEFLAGS illusion and each type of hypoxia.
- State the alcohol, oxygen and post-dive waiting rules.
- Apply IMSAFE and PAVE to a given flight, and walk through DECIDE and 3P for the scenarios.
- Ask questions about anything unclear.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student explains the causes, effects and prevention of spatial disorientation and optical illusions without notes.
- The student identifies the four types of hypoxia with a cause and corrective action for each, and states the 14 CFR 91.211 oxygen requirements.
- The student states the 14 CFR 91.17 alcohol and drug limits and the waiting times after scuba diving.
- The student correctly applies IMSAFE, PAVE, DECIDE and 3P to the lesson scenarios and names the antidote for each hazardous attitude.

### 8. References

- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 2 Aeronautical Decision-Making; Ch. 17 Aeromedical Factors (figures: false horizon 17-22, time of useful consciousness 17-1, Eustachian tube 17-2)
- Risk Management Handbook (FAA-H-8083-2)
