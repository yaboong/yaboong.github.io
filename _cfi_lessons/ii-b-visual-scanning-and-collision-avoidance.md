### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands why see-and-avoid fails and how to scan effectively to detect other aircraft.
- The student knows the right-of-way rules and the practices that reduce collision risk in the air and around the airport.

### 2. Content

- See-and-avoid responsibility (14 CFR 91.113(b)) and traffic advisories (workload permitting)
- Why see-and-avoid fails: environmental conditions, empty-field myopia, accommodation delay, blossom effect, human factors and tunnel vision
- High-risk areas: busy traffic patterns, practice areas, near airports and navaids
- Blind spots of high-wing and low-wing airplanes
- Scanning by phase of flight, the 10-degree / 1-second scan, block scan, binocular vision and windshield posts
- Maneuvering flight and clearing turns
- Right-of-way rules: 91.113 general rules, aircraft in distress, converging, approaching head-on, overtaking, landing (AC 90-66C in the traffic pattern)
- Collision avoidance summary: flight deck management, lights, transponder and ADS-B, ATC services, radio calls

### 3. Schedule (about 45 min)


| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Introduction (NTSB safety alert, see-and-avoid duty) | 5 min |
| Why see-and-avoid fails (vision limits, blossom video) | 10 min |
| How to scan (phases of flight, scan methods, blind spots, clearing turns) | 15 min |
| Right-of-way rules | 10 min |
| Collision avoidance summary and review | 5 min |


### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the slide deck (blossom effect video)
- Whiteboard and markers (right-of-way diagrams)
- Model airplanes for converging, head-on and overtaking scenarios
- Sectional chart and Chart Supplement for local high-traffic areas

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- State the regulatory see-and-avoid duty and the limits of traffic advisories.
- Explain each vision limitation with the photos and the blossom effect video.
- Show high-wing and low-wing blind spots and the scanning technique for each phase of flight.
- Demonstrate the block scan and explain clearing turns before maneuvers.
- Use model airplanes to quiz the student on each right-of-way rule.
- Summarize practical ways to reduce collision risk.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain empty-field myopia, accommodation delay and the blossom effect in his or her own words.
- Describe a proper scan pattern and when to make clearing turns.
- Decide who has the right-of-way in each model-airplane scenario.
- List the equipment and procedures used to reduce collision risk.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student explains at least four reasons see-and-avoid fails and how to counter each.
- The student describes an effective scan (short, regularly spaced eye movements of no more than 10 degrees, about 1 second each) and the scan for each phase of flight.
- The student correctly applies the 14 CFR 91.113 right-of-way rules to all lesson scenarios.

### 8. References

- 14 CFR 91.111 (operating near other aircraft), 91.113 (right-of-way rules: except water operations)
- AC 90-48 (Pilots' Role in Collision Avoidance)
- AC 90-66 (Non-Towered Airport Flight Operations)
- AIM Ch. 8, Section 1, Vision in Flight (8-1-6) and Judgment Aspects of Collision Avoidance (8-1-8)
- Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3), Ch. 1 (Collision Avoidance) and clearing procedures
- NTSB Safety Alert SA-058 (See and Avoid)
