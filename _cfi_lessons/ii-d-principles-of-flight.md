### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands the four forces, how a wing produces lift, the types of drag, and how lift and drag relate.
- The student can explain stalls, load factor, stability and left-turning tendencies, and apply them to the PA-28-161.

### 2. Content

- Four forces (lift, weight, thrust, drag) and their balance in steady flight, climbs and descents
- Airfoil terms, angle of attack (AoA) and the AoA indicator, center of pressure, camber vs symmetric airfoils
- How lift is produced: flow turning, Bernoulli's principle, Newton's third law; why the equal transit theory is wrong; the lift equation
- Drag: parasite (form, interference, skin friction) and induced drag; wingtip vortices (linked from Wake Turbulence)
- Lift/drag ratio, L/D max and best glide speed, effect of weight on best glide speed
- Stall and critical AoA, airflow separation
- Load factor, its effect on stall speed (PA-28-161 POH stall speed chart) and the V-g diagram (linked from Maneuvering Speed)
- Stability, maneuverability and controllability; lateral, longitudinal, directional, static and dynamic stability
- Left-turning tendencies (linked from the Left Turning Tendencies module)

### 3. Schedule (about 60 min)


| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Introduction and four forces | 10 min |
| Lift (airfoil, AoA, Bernoulli and Newton, lift equation) | 15 min |
| Drag and L/D (best glide) | 10 min |
| Stall and load factor | 10 min |
| Stability and left-turning tendencies | 10 min |
| Review | 5 min |


### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the slide deck (wind tunnel, Newton and Bernoulli videos, NASA FoilSim screenshots)
- Whiteboard and markers (force diagrams, drag curve)
- Model airplane (axes, AoA, stability)
- PA-28-161 POH (stall speed chart)

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Draw the four forces and explain their balance in level flight, climb and descent.
- Define airfoil terms and AoA, and show the difference between AoA and pitch attitude.
- Use the videos to explain flow turning, Bernoulli and Newton, and why the equal transit theory is wrong.
- Draw the drag curve and locate L/D max and best glide speed; explain the effect of weight.
- Explain the stall as an AoA problem, then load factor and stall speed with the POH chart.
- Demonstrate the three axes and the types of stability with a model airplane.
- Ask the student to explain each concept back.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Draw and label the four forces and an airfoil.
- Explain how a wing produces lift and what the lift equation shows.
- Identify parasite and induced drag on the drag curve and explain best glide speed.
- Read the stall speed for a given bank angle from the POH chart.
- Explain each type of stability and the left-turning tendencies.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student explains lift, drag, stall, load factor and stability correctly without notes.
- The student explains that a stall occurs when the critical AoA is exceeded, at any airspeed, attitude or power setting.
- The student correctly uses the PA-28-161 stall speed chart for a given weight and bank angle.

### 8. References

- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 4 Principles of Flight; Ch. 5 Aerodynamics of Flight (figures 5-10 and 5-11 wingtip vortices; 5-38 V-g diagram)

- PA-28-161 Pilot's Operating Handbook, Section 5 (stall speed chart)
