One lesson plan covering three decks: Part 1 (flight controls, brakes, landing gear, oil), Part 2 (fuel, powerplant, environmental, propeller, electrical) and Part 3 (instruments, avionics).

### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands how each PA-28-161 system works, how the systems connect, and how each one can fail.
- The student can explain the pilot's controls and indications for each system, using the POH and the airplane itself.

### 2. Content

- Part 1: primary and secondary flight controls (stabilator, antiservo tab, flaps, trim) compared with the Cessna 172; adverse yaw; toe brakes and the hydraulic disc brake system; fixed tricycle gear, oleo struts, strut exposure, tire pressure, nosewheel steering; oil system functions and limits
- Part 2: Lycoming O-320-D3G; fuel system (low-wing fuel-pump system, usable fuel, vents, 100LL); float-type carburetor (mixture, venturi, throttle); four-stroke cycle; dual magnetos, P-lead and hot mag; crankshaft as the hub; fixed-pitch and constant-speed propellers; primer vs fuel injection; carburetor icing and carb heat; cabin heat, defroster and carbon monoxide; starter, alternator, battery, ammeter, circuit breakers; alternator failure case study
- Part 3: pitot-static system and blockages; altimeter (setting, pressure and temperature errors), VSI and airspeed indicator with PA-28-161 markings; alternate static source; vacuum system and gyroscopic principles; attitude, heading and turn instruments; magnetic compass errors (variation, deviation, UNOS, ANDS); N79NA avionics (G5 PFD/HSI, ADC, AHRS, GNC 355, GMA 342, transponder, ADS-B, WAAS check) and antenna locations

### 3. Schedule (about 2 hours 30 min; can be split into three sessions)


| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Part 1: flight controls, brakes, landing gear, oil | 30 min |
| Part 2: fuel, carburetor, ignition, crankshaft, propeller | 35 min |
| Part 2: primer and fuel injection, carb icing, environmental, starter, electrical | 25 min |
| Part 3: pitot-static instruments and errors | 25 min |
| Part 3: gyroscopic instruments, magnetic compass | 20 min |
| Part 3: avionics and antennas, overall review | 15 min |


### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the three decks (engine, gyro and compass videos)
- PA-28-161 POH and school checklist
- The airplane (N79NA) for a walk-around: control surfaces, struts, engine compartment, antennas, panel
- Whiteboard and markers (engine flow diagram, pitot-static diagram)
- Garmin G5 and GNC 355 manuals; GNC 355 trainer app on an iPad

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Build the engine flow diagram one piece at a time (fuel → carburetor → cylinder → magneto → crankshaft → propeller), asking the student to predict the next piece.
- Ask the two key questions: where is the electrical system in the engine flow, and what turns the crankshaft first.
- Relate every system to its cockpit control, indication and POH page.
- For each instrument, explain the source (pitot, static, vacuum, electric) and have the student work out blockage and failure effects instead of memorizing them.
- Show the N79NA panel and avionics, and walk the airplane to locate parts and antennas.
- Use the alternator failure flight to discuss failure recognition and response.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain each system's purpose, main parts, cockpit controls and indications.
- Draw the engine flow and explain how the fuel, ignition and propeller systems connect.
- Work out instrument indications for pitot or static blockage and for use of the alternate static source.
- Apply variation and compass turning and acceleration errors to examples.
- Locate the system parts, controls and antennas on the airplane.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student explains the operation, normal indications and common failure modes of every PA-28-161 system covered, using the POH correctly.
- The student correctly predicts instrument indications for each pitot-static blockage and gyro failure.
- The student explains the pilot actions for carburetor icing, a hot mag, carbon monoxide and an alternator failure.

### 8. References

- PA-28-161 Pilot's Operating Handbook: Section 2 Limitations; Section 4 Normal Procedures (4.31 Stopping Engine); Section 7 Description and Operation of the Airplane and Its Systems (7-18 alternate static source); Section 8 Handling, Servicing and Maintenance
- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 6 Flight Controls; Ch. 7 Aircraft Systems; Ch. 8 Flight Instruments
- Instrument Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-15), Ch. 5 Flight Instruments
- AIM Ch. 4, Section 1 (transponder and ADS-B Out operation); 14 CFR 91.215 (ATC transponder), 91.225 (ADS-B Out)
