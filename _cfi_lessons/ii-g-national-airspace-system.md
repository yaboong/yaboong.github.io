### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student knows the dimensions, entry and equipment requirements, speed limits and VFR weather minimums of each airspace class.
- The student can identify special use airspace and other airspace areas on a sectional chart and knows the rules for operating in or near them.

### 2. Content

- Airspace classes A, B, C, D, E and G: floors, ceilings and typical sizes
- Entry and equipment requirements (clearance or two-way communication, transponder, ADS-B Out, pilot certificate)
- Speed limits (14 CFR 91.117) and VFR weather minimums (14 CFR 91.155)
- Airspace depiction on sectional charts; San Diego Class B example
- Special use airspace (MCPRAWN): MOA, controlled firing area, prohibited, restricted, alert, warning areas, national security area
- Other airspace areas: ADIZ, military training routes, parachute jump areas, TRSA, wilderness and park areas, Washington DC FRZ, TFRs (VIP and sporting event examples)
- Special Air Traffic Rules (14 CFR Part 93) and SFRAs: Washington DC, Grand Canyon, Hudson and East River Exclusion, Los Angeles
- Appendix on Class E: why it exists, floors (surface, 700 ft AGL, 1,200 ft AGL, zipper line, above 1,200 ft), Class G under blue vignettes

### 3. Schedule (about 1 hour 30 min)


| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Airspace classes, requirements, speed limits | 20 min |
| VFR weather minimums and chart depiction | 15 min |
| Special use airspace | 15 min |
| Other airspace areas, TFRs, SFRAs | 20 min |
| Class E deep dive (appendix) | 15 min |
| Review with the sectional chart | 5 min |


### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck
- Current sectional chart (New York) and terminal area chart; ForeFlight
- Aeronautical Chart Users' Guide
- Whiteboard and markers (airspace profile drawing)

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Draw the airspace profile (the "wedding cake") and build each class's requirements on it.
- Use the VFR weather minimums figure to show a simple way to remember the numbers.
- Point to each airspace type on a real sectional chart and ask the student to identify it.
- Explain why Class E exists (protecting IFR traffic) rather than only listing its floors.
- Show real TFR NOTAMs and explain how to check for them before every flight.

### 6. Student's Actions

- State the dimensions, requirements and VFR minimums for each airspace class.
- Identify airspace classes, special use airspace and other areas on the sectional chart, including Class E floors.
- Decode Class B shelf numbers and special use airspace labels.
- Explain how to find and comply with TFRs and SFRA procedures.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student correctly states each airspace class's dimensions, entry and equipment requirements, speed limits and VFR weather minimums.
- The student identifies every airspace type on a sectional chart and explains the operating rules for it.
- The student explains how to check for TFRs and the actions required before entering special use airspace.

### 8. References

- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 15 Airspace
- Aeronautical Information Manual: Ch. 3 Airspace (3-2 controlled airspace, 3-4 special use airspace, 3-5 other airspace areas); 5-6 national security and interception procedures
- 14 CFR 91.117 (aircraft speed), 91.126 through 91.135 (operations in Class G, E, D, C, B and A airspace), 91.137 through 91.145 (temporary flight restrictions), 91.155 (basic VFR weather minimums), 91.215 and 91.225 (transponder and ADS-B Out); Part 93 (Special Air Traffic Rules); Part 99 (Security Control of Air Traffic)
- FAA Aeronautical Chart Users' Guide (VFR charts, airspace symbols)
- Current sectional and terminal area charts
