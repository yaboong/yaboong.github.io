### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands how ground-based and satellite navigation systems work, their limitations, and how to check that they are usable.
- The student can use the VOR receiver, CDI and HSI correctly, and can request and use ATC radar services with the transponder and ADS-B.

### 2. Content

- Ground-based (NDB/ADF, VOR/DME, TACAN, VORTAC) and satellite (GNSS, GPS) systems compared
- DME operation and slant range error
- VOR: Victor airways, principle of operation (reference and variable signals), identification by Morse code
- VOR receiver parts: course index, OBS, TO/FROM indicator, CDI needle; HSI
- Orientation, TO/FROM, reverse sensing on a CDI, and why an HSI has no reverse sensing
- VOR limitations: line of sight, unusable radials (ALB example in the Chart Supplement), cone of confusion, reverse sensing
- Finding VORs in the Chart Supplement; service volume classes; checking NOTAMs for out-of-service VORs
- VOR checks (VOT, ground and airborne checkpoints, dual check) and the 30-day IFR requirement
- GPS: how it works, RAIM, SBAS/WAAS, primary vs supplemental navigation
- Radar services (shared slides): VFR traffic advisories and flight following phraseology, transponder codes and modes, ADS-B Out and In, the PA-28-161's equipment and antennas, managing distraction

### 3. Schedule (about 1 hour 45 min)


| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Navigation systems overview, NDB, DME | 15 min |
| VOR principle, receiver parts, orientation | 20 min |
| Reverse sensing and the HSI (simulator) | 15 min |
| VOR limitations, Chart Supplement, service volumes, NOTAMs | 15 min |
| VOR checks | 10 min |
| GPS, RAIM, WAAS | 10 min |
| Radar services, transponder, ADS-B, flight following | 15 min |
| Review | 5 min |


### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck and a VOR simulator
- Current sectional chart and Chart Supplement (Northeast)
- FAA NOTAM Search
- PA-28-161 panel (G5 HSI, GNC 355, Garmin 255 NAV/COM, Stratus ESG transponder) and an iPad with ForeFlight
- Whiteboard and markers

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Compare the systems by asking who finds the direction: the aircraft (NDB) or the station (VOR).
- Demonstrate VOR orientation on the simulator, then have the student set up each case.
- Show reverse sensing on a CDI and the same setup on the HSI.
- Walk through the Chart Supplement, service volumes and NOTAMs for VORs along a real route.
- Explain the VOR check tolerances and how to log a check.
- Role-play a flight following request from initial call to handoff.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain how each navigation system works and its main limitation.
- Determine position (radial, TO/FROM) from receiver indications and correct a reverse sensing setup.
- Look up a VOR's frequency, service volume and unusable radials, and check its NOTAMs.
- State the VOR check tolerances and the 30-day rule.
- Explain RAIM and WAAS and the difference between primary and supplemental GPS.
- Make a flight following request and set the transponder correctly.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student explains the operation and limitations of VOR, DME and GPS and how to verify that they are usable.
- The student correctly interprets CDI and HSI indications, including reverse sensing.
- The student correctly requests VFR flight following and uses the transponder and ADS-B equipment in the PA-28-161.
