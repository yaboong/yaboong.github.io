### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands how the eye works at night, the illusions that occur at night, and how to protect night vision.
- The student knows the night equipment and lighting requirements and can plan and fly a night flight safely, including currency requirements and risk management.

### 2. Content

- Definitions of "night" in different regulations (position lights, logging night time, carrying passengers)
- Night vision: rods and cones, the night blind spot, off-center viewing, dark adaptation, factors that degrade night vision (altitude, smoking, alcohol, medication, fatigue, diet)
- Night illusions: autokinesis, false horizon, black hole and other approach illusions
- Night VFR equipment (14 CFR 91.205(c), FLAPS), aircraft lights by phase of flight, position lights and reading another airplane's direction
- Personal equipment (flashlights, spare batteries) and obstruction lights
- Night flight sequence: planning (weather, lighted checkpoints, route), engine start, taxi and run-up, takeoff and climb, en route (clouds, fog, flight over water), pilot-controlled lighting, pattern, round out and landing
- Currency (61.57(b)) versus proficiency
- Night risks (shared slides): runway incursion, distraction and task prioritization, illusions, collision hazards

### 3. Schedule (about 1 hour 30 min)


| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Definitions of night | 5 min |
| Night vision and dark adaptation | 20 min |
| Night illusions | 15 min |
| Equipment, aircraft lights, obstruction lights | 15 min |
| Night flight from planning to landing | 25 min |
| Currency vs proficiency, risk review | 10 min |


### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck (illusion photos, runway glare video)
- PA-28-161 POH and night checklist
- Flashlights (white and red), spare batteries
- Sectional chart and Chart Supplement (pilot-controlled lighting frequencies, airport lighting)
- N79NA for a lighting walk-around

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Explain rods and cones with the PHAK figures and demonstrate off-center viewing.
- Show each illusion photo and ask what the pilot would feel and how to counter it.
- Have the student list the FLAPS items and check the lights on the airplane.
- Walk through a full night flight phase by phase, explaining what is different from day flying.
- Explain the difference between being current and being proficient, and help the student set night personal minimums.
- Review the night risk slides and discuss mitigation for each.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain dark adaptation, the night blind spot and off-center viewing, and how to protect night vision.
- Identify the night illusions and the correct response to each.
- List the night VFR equipment and the lights used in each phase of flight.
- Determine another airplane's direction from its position lights.
- Plan a night flight, including lighted checkpoints and pilot-controlled lighting frequencies.
- State the night currency requirement for carrying passengers.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student explains night vision, dark adaptation and night illusions, and the techniques to counter them.
- The student correctly states the night equipment, lighting and currency requirements.
- The student plans a night flight that addresses weather, route, lighting and personal minimums, and explains the procedures for each phase of flight.
