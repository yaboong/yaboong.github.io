### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands why supplemental oxygen is needed at altitude, the oxygen rules, and how to recognize and prevent hypoxia.
- The student understands how oxygen systems and cabin pressurization work, and how to recognize and respond to a decompression.

### 2. Content

- Oxygen percentage vs pressure at altitude; hypoxic hypoxia
- Oxygen rules (14 CFR 91.211): 12,500, 14,000 and 15,000 ft cabin pressure altitude; rules for pressurized cabins
- Hypoxia signs and symptoms (AC 61-107B) and why hypoxia is missed (euphoria, slow onset, individual differences)
- Time of useful consciousness
- Oxygen delivery systems: continuous flow, diluter demand, pressure demand, electronic pulse demand; cannulas and masks
- Oxygen equipment preflight and passenger briefing (PRICE)
- Pressurization: how air enters and leaves the cabin, isobaric and differential ranges, cabin instruments (cabin rate of climb, cabin altitude and differential pressure)
- Decompression: explosive, rapid and gradual; causes; effects; immediate actions (oxygen mask on, emergency descent)

### 3. Schedule (about 1 hour)


| Segment | Time |
|---|---|
| Why oxygen is needed, oxygen rules | 15 min |
| Hypoxia, time of useful consciousness | 10 min |
| Oxygen systems and PRICE | 10 min |
| Pressurization and cabin instruments | 10 min |
| Decompression and response | 10 min |
| Review | 5 min |


### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck
- 14 CFR 91.211 text and AC 61-107B
- Sample oxygen equipment (cannula, mask, portable bottle) if available
- Pulse oximeter
- Whiteboard and markers (pressurization range graph)

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Explain why oxygen stays 21% while its partial pressure drops, using the pressure numbers on the slide.
- Have the student apply 91.211 to sample flights at different altitudes and durations.
- Stress why hypoxia is hard to notice, and recommend altitude chamber training.
- Compare the oxygen delivery systems and demonstrate the PRICE check.
- Draw the isobaric and differential ranges, then explain the types of decompression and the immediate actions.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain hypoxic hypoxia and the factors that affect it.
- Apply the 91.211 oxygen rules to sample flights.
- List hypoxia signs and symptoms and explain time of useful consciousness.
- Perform a PRICE check and describe a passenger oxygen briefing.
- Explain how pressurization works and the actions after a decompression.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student correctly applies the supplemental oxygen requirements.
- The student explains the recognition and prevention of hypoxia and the operation of the oxygen systems covered.
- The student explains how cabin pressurization works, the types and effects of decompression, and the correct pilot response.

### 8. References

- 14 CFR 91.211 (supplemental oxygen); 61.31(g) (additional training required for pressurized aircraft)
- AC 61-107B (Aircraft Operations at Altitudes Above 25,000 Feet MSL and/or Mach Numbers Greater Than .75)
- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 7 Aircraft Systems (oxygen systems, pressurized aircraft); Ch. 17 Aeromedical Factors (hypoxia, time of useful consciousness)
- Aeronautical Information Manual: Ch. 8, Section 1 (8-1-2 effects of altitude)
- FAA Pilot Safety Brochure: Hypoxia; FAA altitude chamber training (Civil Aerospace Medical Institute)
