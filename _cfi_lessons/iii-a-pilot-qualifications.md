### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student knows the certificates and ratings issued under Part 61 and the eligibility, aeronautical experience, privileges and limitations of each.
- The student can determine whether a pilot is current and qualified for a given flight (flight review, recent experience, additional PIC endorsements, medical or BasicMed, required documents) and understands that being current is not the same as being proficient.

### 2. Content

- Certificates and ratings (category, class, type, instrument) (61.5)
- Student pilot: eligibility, pre-solo knowledge test and maneuvers, solo and night solo endorsements, 90-day rule; solo cross-country rules and endorsements (61.83, 61.87, 61.93)
- Eligibility and aeronautical experience: sport, recreational, private, commercial, flight instructor (incl. spin endorsement)
- Certificate duration (61.19); flight instructor recent experience (61.197)
- Flight review (61.56); recent flight experience: day/night passenger currency, instrument currency, IPC (61.57)
- Logbook requirements (61.51); flight instructor records (61.189)
- Privileges and limitations: student, sport, recreational, private (expense sharing), commercial, flight instructor (61.89, 61.315–61.331, 61.101, 61.113, 61.133, 61.193, 61.195)
- Additional PIC requirements: type rating, complex, high-performance, pressurized, tailwheel (61.31)
- Medical certificates: which class for which operation, duration by class and age, medical deficiency (61.23, 61.53); BasicMed (61.23(c)(3), 61.113(i), Part 68)
- Required documents and who may inspect them (61.3); logbook carriage (61.51(i))
- Risk management: qualification vs currency vs proficiency, rental airplane case study

### 3. Schedule (about 2 hours)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Certificates and ratings, student pilot rules, solo cross-country | 20 min |
| Eligibility and aeronautical experience by certificate | 20 min |
| Duration, flight review, recent experience, logbooks | 20 min |
| Privileges and limitations, additional PIC endorsements | 25 min |
| Medical certificates and BasicMed | 15 min |
| Required documents, risk management, scenario review | 20 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck
- Current FAR/AIM or eCFR (14 CFR Part 61, Part 68)
- Student's and instructor's logbooks, pilot and medical certificates (as examples)
- Whiteboard and markers

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Teach each topic from the regulation itself, showing where to find it in Part 61.
- Use the student's own logbook to check flight review, passenger currency and instrument currency dates.
- Give "Can this pilot make this flight?" scenarios (certificate, ratings, endorsements, currency, medical) and ask the student to cite the rule.
- Explain the difference between currency and proficiency with the case study, and help the student set personal minimums.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Find and explain the relevant Part 61 section for each question.
- Determine their own currency and endorsement status from their logbook.
- Work through the eligibility and privilege scenarios and state the limiting rule.
- Identify the documents to carry and who may ask to see them.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student correctly states the eligibility, experience, privileges and limitations for the certificates covered.
- The student correctly determines whether a pilot is current and qualified for sample flights, citing the regulation.
- The student explains the medical and BasicMed requirements and the required documents.
- The student explains why currency alone does not equal proficiency.

### 8. References

- 14 CFR Part 61: 61.3, 61.5, 61.19, 61.23, 61.31, 61.51, 61.53, 61.56, 61.57; Subpart C (61.83–61.101); Subparts D, E, F (61.103–61.133); Subpart H (61.181–61.199); Subpart J (61.301–61.331)
- 14 CFR Part 68 (Requirements for Operating Certain Small Aircraft Without a Medical Certificate)

- Risk Management Handbook (FAA-H-8083-2), Ch. 3 (assessing risk; currency vs proficiency)
- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 1 Introduction to Flying (pilot certifications)
