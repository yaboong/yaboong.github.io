### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands what makes an airplane airworthy, the required documents, inspections and maintenance records, and who may perform and approve maintenance.
- The student can decide whether N79NA is airworthy for a flight, including flying with inoperative equipment and when a special flight permit is needed.

### 2. Content

- Definition of airworthy (conforms to type design and in condition for safe operation); owner/operator and PIC responsibilities (91.403, 91.7)
- Type certificate, TCDS (2A13), STCs and ADs; standard and special airworthiness certificates (21.175)
- Required documents (ARROW); N79NA's actual airworthiness certificate, registration (7 years, 47.41) and weight and balance record (43.5(c))
- Required inspections (AV1ATES): ADs, 30-day VOR check, annual/100-hour (91.409), altimeter-static (91.411), transponder (91.413), ELT (91.207), STC inspections
- AD vs SAIB; N79NA's AD compliance report and VOR check log; reading the airframe and avionics logbooks
- Part 43 maintenance: major, minor and preventive maintenance; who may perform (43.3) and approve return to service (43.7); record entries (43.9); Form 337
- Preventive maintenance by pilots (Part 43 Appendix A(c))
- Required equipment: day VFR and night VFR (91.205)
- Inoperative equipment: MEL, KOEL, 91.213(d) decision path, deactivate and placard; MEL structure example
- Special flight permits (21.197, 21.199)
- Risk management: options when equipment is found inoperative, combined effects of deferrals

### 3. Schedule (about 1 hour 45 min)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Airworthiness definition, TC/TCDS, airworthiness certificates | 15 min |
| Required documents with N79NA's paperwork | 15 min |
| Required inspections, ADs, logbook review | 30 min |
| Part 43 maintenance and preventive maintenance | 15 min |
| Required equipment, inoperative equipment, special flight permit | 20 min |
| Risk scenarios and review | 10 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck
- N79NA's airframe, engine and avionics logbooks, AD compliance report, VOR check log, Form 337s
- N79NA itself (documents on board, placards)
- PA-28-161 POH (Section 2 limitations, Section 6 weight and balance, equipment list)
- Current FAR/AIM (14 CFR Parts 21, 39, 43, 47, 91)
- Whiteboard and markers

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Define airworthy with the two conditions and tie each later topic back to them.
- Show N79NA's real documents and logbooks and have the student find each required inspection.
- Explain AD types and how to confirm compliance from the records.
- Walk through the 91.213(d) decision path with real inoperative-equipment examples.
- Give preflight scenarios (expired inspection, inoperative light, overdue AD) and ask for a go/no-go decision with the regulation.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Locate the required documents in the airplane and state their requirements.
- Find the dates of the last annual, 100-hour, altimeter/static, transponder and ELT inspections and the AD status in N79NA's records.
- List the day and night VFR required equipment.
- Apply 91.213(d) to inoperative-equipment scenarios, including placarding.
- Name preventive maintenance a pilot may do and how to log it.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student correctly determines whether N79NA is airworthy from its documents and logbooks.
- The student correctly states inspection intervals and AD compliance requirements.
- The student correctly decides whether flight is permitted with inoperative equipment, and when a special flight permit is needed.
- The student explains who may perform and approve maintenance, including pilot preventive maintenance.
