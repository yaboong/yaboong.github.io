### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands the basic meteorology behind weather (atmosphere, stability, moisture, clouds, wind, air masses and fronts) and the weather hazards to flight.
- The student can obtain and interpret weather reports, forecasts and advisories, and use them with personal minimums to make a go/no-go decision.

### 2. Content

- Part 1: atmosphere composition and layers, standard atmosphere and lapse rates; stability and inversions
- Heat transfer, moisture, relative humidity, dew point and spread; cloud types, families and lifting; cloud base estimate; precipitation types
- Wind: pressure gradient, Coriolis, highs and lows, friction; local winds; wind shear ([Wind Shear]({{ '/cfi-lesson-plan/module-wind-shear/' | relative_url }}) module); mountain wave
- Air masses and fronts: classification, frontal weather, warm vs cold front, stationary and occluded fronts
- Hazards: turbulence (incl. clear air turbulence, rough-air procedure with Va), thunderstorms (ingredients, life cycle, types, avoidance, microbursts), icing (types, where found, effects, carburetor ice), fog types, frost, obstructions to visibility
- Part 2: weather sources (AWC, NWS, FAA) and preflight information (91.103); Flight Service and briefing types
- Airport weather: ATIS, AWOS, ASOS; METAR and SPECI decoding; TAF; PIREPs
- En route: winds and temperatures aloft, SPC convective outlook, surface analysis and prog charts, radar and its limits, freezing level/icing/turbulence graphics, flight categories
- In-flight advisories: AIRMET/G-AIRMET, SIGMET, Convective SIGMET; weather updates in flight (Flight Service, ADS-B In weather limits)
- Personal minimums table ([Personal Minimums]({{ '/cfi-lesson-plan/module-personal-minimums/' | relative_url }}) module)

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with both decks (videos)
- aviationweather.gov, ForeFlight, 1-800-WX-BRIEF / 1800wxbrief.com
- FAA Aviation Weather Handbook figures; printed METAR/TAF examples for decoding practice
- ATIS copy form
- Whiteboard and markers (frontal cross-sections, stability diagram)

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Build cause and effect: explain how stability and moisture produce clouds, precipitation and hazards, instead of memorizing lists.
- Draw warm and cold front cross-sections and relate them to the METAR sequence on the slides.
- Decode one METAR, TAF and PIREP together, then have the student decode new ones from the current day.
- Show live products on aviationweather.gov and ForeFlight and point out their limits (radar latency, automated graphics).
- Finish with a real briefing for a planned flight and a go/no-go decision against personal minimums.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain stability, frontal weather and the conditions for thunderstorms, icing and fog.
- Decode METARs, SPECIs, TAFs, PIREPs and winds aloft without help.
- Read prog charts, convective outlooks, G-AIRMETs and SIGMETs and explain their meaning for a route.
- Obtain a standard briefing and make a go/no-go decision using personal minimums.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student explains the meteorology behind common weather and its hazards to a PA-28-161.
- The student correctly decodes and interprets the reports, forecasts and advisories covered.
- The student obtains a complete weather briefing and makes a reasoned go/no-go decision, including how to get updates in flight.

### 8. References

- Aviation Weather Handbook (FAA-H-8083-28): Part 1 weather theory (atmosphere, heat and temperature, wind, moisture, clouds, air masses and fronts, turbulence, icing, thunderstorms, fog, mountain weather); Part 2 weather products (METAR, TAF, PIREP, winds aloft, AIRMET/SIGMET, analysis and prog charts)
- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 12 Weather Theory; Ch. 13 Aviation Weather Services
- Aeronautical Information Manual: Ch. 7, Section 1 Meteorology (Flight Service briefings, in-flight weather advisories, PIREPs, weather radar, FIS-B)
- 14 CFR 91.103 (preflight action)
- aviationweather.gov and the Storm Prediction Center product pages
