### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student can perform a chandelle — a maximum-performance 180° climbing turn — with the correct pitch and bank sequence, finishing just above power-on stall speed.

### 2. Content

- What a chandelle is: first 90° constant bank with increasing pitch, second 90° constant pitch with decreasing bank
- Setup (CRAAC): clearing, references, entry speed at or below Va, altitude floor, configuration
- AFH figure walk-through (points A–E)
- Procedure flow and control pressures as airspeed decreases (right rudder, aileron against overbanking, back pressure in the rollout)
- Common errors

### 3. Schedule (ground about 30 min; flight about 40 min)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Definition and AFH figure | 10 min |
| Setup and procedure | 10 min |
| Control pressures and common errors | 10 min |
| Flight: demonstration and practice | 40 min |
| Postflight debrief | 10 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck
- PA-28-161 POH (maneuvering speed, stall speeds) and school checklist
- PA-28-161; model airplane

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Explain the two halves and why pitch at the 90° point decides the result.
- Demonstrate in both directions; point out rudder needs as airspeed decreases.
- Coach a constant-rate rollout to finish just above stall speed.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Set up at the entry speed and altitude with a clear 90° and 180° reference.
- Fly the maneuver with constant bank then constant pitch, rolling out at 180° just above stall speed.
- Recover with minimum altitude loss.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student establishes about 30° bank, reaches maximum pitch at the 90° point, rolls out at 180° ±10° just above power-on stall speed, and stays coordinated, finishing no lower than 1,500 ft AGL.

### 8. References

- Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3), Ch. 10 Performance Maneuvers (chandelles, common errors)
- PA-28-161 Pilot's Operating Handbook: Section 2 (maneuvering speed), Section 5 (stall speeds)
- Commercial Pilot Airman Certification Standards (chandelles)
