### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student can fly eights on pylons by holding the pylon on the visual reference line with altitude changes at the pivotal altitude, showing division of attention and precise control.

### 2. Content

- Purpose and AFH figure; comparison with turns around a point
- Pivotal altitude: concept, formula (groundspeed² ÷ 11.3 + pylon elevation), table for local conditions; correction with altitude, not bank
- Above/below pivotal altitude and the correction; no drift correction in the turn; bank set by distance
- Visual reference line in the PA-28-161 (left seat cues); rudder for coordination only
- Maneuver: altitude/airspeed and pylon selection, finding the wind (HSI wind marker), entry, each pylon's turn, full flow
- Video; common errors; risks (links to human factors, collision avoidance, [low altitude]({{ '/cfi-lesson-plan/module-low-altitude-maneuvering-cfit/' | relative_url }}), [stalls/spins]({{ '/cfi-lesson-plan/x-c-i-stalls-spin/' | relative_url }}), [emergency operations]({{ '/cfi-lesson-plan/xii-a-d-emergency-operations/' | relative_url }}))

### 3. Schedule (ground about 50 min; flight about 45 min)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose and pivotal altitude | 15 min |
| Corrections and visual reference line | 15 min |
| Maneuver flow, pylon selection, wind | 15 min |
| Video, errors, risks | 5 min |
| Flight: demonstration and practice | 45 min |
| Postflight debrief | 10 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck (video)
- Sectional chart (pylon and terrain elevation), calculator for pivotal altitude
- PA-28-161 (G5 groundspeed, HSI wind marker)

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Compute pivotal altitude with the student for the day's wind.
- Show the reference line from the left seat and explain forward/back corrections.
- Demonstrate, then coach altitude corrections and rudder discipline.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Choose pylons, determine the wind and compute pivotal altitudes.
- Enter correctly and hold each pylon on the reference line with altitude changes.
- Correct for wind between pylons, not in the turn.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student holds each pylon on the reference line with only minor deviations, keeps the turn coordinated, and stays at a safe altitude and airspeed throughout.

### 8. References

- Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3), Ch. 7 Ground Reference Maneuvers (eights on pylons, pivotal altitude, common errors)
- Commercial Pilot Airman Certification Standards (eights on pylons)
- Sectional chart for pylon and terrain elevations
