### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student can communicate correctly with ATC and on CTAF through a whole flight, using standard phraseology, readbacks and the transponder.
- The student knows how to handle a radio failure (troubleshooting, 7600, NORDO arrival, light gun signals), when and how to declare an emergency, and how to identify airport lighting.

### 2. Content

- Scenario: day VFR cross-country Santa Maria (KSMX) to Bakersfield (KBFL), followed through every radio call
- Finding frequencies: sectional, Chart Supplement, ForeFlight, ATC handoffs
- VFR flight following (traffic advisories): what it is, workload permitting, see and avoid still applies
- Transponder codes and modes; 7500, 7600, 7700
- ATC facilities: Ground, Tower, TRACON, ARTCC and the order of handoffs
- Radio call structure (who, where, what)
- Departure: Ground call with ATIS, taxi and hold short readback; Tower takeoff clearance and readback
- Approach and Center: requesting flight following, squawk and ident, radar contact, traffic advisories and responses, frequency changes and check-ins
- Lost communication scenario with the DECIDE model (troubleshooting, terrain blocking VHF, climbing, 7600, transmitting in the blind)
- Arrival: Approach to Tower handoff, pattern instructions, landing clearance, exit and taxi, runway crossing
- Non-towered CTAF self-announcing (AC 90-66C) and pattern entries
- Real radio failure: land or divert, 14 CFR 91.185 (VFR), NORDO arrival and acknowledging light signals
- Light gun signals ([Light Signals]({{ '/cfi-lesson-plan/module-light-signals/' | relative_url }}) module), declaring an emergency
- Airport and runway lighting ([Airport Lights]({{ '/cfi-lesson-plan/module-airport-lights/' | relative_url }}) module); pilot-controlled lighting
- Common communication, lighting and transponder errors; reference call sets for local airports

### 3. Schedule (about 2 hours)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Frequencies, flight following, transponder, ATC facilities | 20 min |
| Departure and en route calls (Ground, Tower, Approach, Center) | 25 min |
| Lost communication scenario and real radio failure | 25 min |
| Arrival and non-towered CTAF calls | 20 min |
| Light gun signals, declaring an emergency, airport lighting | 20 min |
| Common errors and radio role-play | 10 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck
- Sectional chart, Chart Supplement, ForeFlight
- Handheld radio or headset for role-play; light gun signal card
- PA-28-161 POH (Section 3 emergency procedures, electrical failure)
- Whiteboard and markers

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Play the role of each ATC facility while the student makes every call in the scenario flight.
- Correct phraseology and readbacks immediately, and explain why each element matters.
- Walk through the lost communication scenario with DECIDE, asking the student for each decision.
- Quiz light gun signals for an aircraft on the ground and in flight, and the matching pilot acknowledgment.
- Show airport lighting with photos and explain how to set pilot-controlled lighting.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Make all calls from engine start to shutdown for towered and non-towered airports with correct readbacks.
- Request flight following and respond to traffic advisories correctly.
- Troubleshoot a radio problem and state the radio failure procedures, including the transponder code.
- Interpret light gun signals and airport lighting.
- Declare an emergency with the correct information.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student communicates clearly and correctly with ATC and on CTAF using standard phraseology and readbacks.
- The student correctly states and applies radio failure procedures, transponder codes and light gun signals.
- The student identifies airport and runway lighting and knows when and how to declare an emergency.

### 8. References

- Aeronautical Information Manual: Ch. 4, Section 1 (services available to pilots, including radar traffic advisories, transponder and ADS-B operation); Section 2 Radio Communications Phraseology and Techniques; Section 3 Airport Operations (traffic control light signals); Ch. 6, Section 3 (distress and urgency procedures) and Section 4 (two-way radio communications failure); Ch. 2, Section 1 Airport Lighting Aids; Pilot/Controller Glossary
- 14 CFR 91.125 (ATC light signals), 91.185 (two-way radio communications failure), 91.215 (ATC transponder)
- AC 90-66C (Non-Towered Airport Flight Operations)
- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 14 Airport Operations (radio communications, ATC light signals, airport lighting)
- Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3), Ch. 8 Airport Traffic Patterns (entries)
- Chart Supplement and sectional charts for the scenario airports
