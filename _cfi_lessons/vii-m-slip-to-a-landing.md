### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands forward slips and sideslips and can use a forward slip to lose excess altitude on approach without gaining airspeed, then recover and land.

### 2. Content

- What a slip is: excess bank for the rate of turn, cross-controlled, more drag, less vertical lift; uses of a slip
- Video of a slip on approach
- Forward slip vs sideslip: same controls, different purpose and result (AFH figures)
- Forward slip procedure: entry, during the slip (pitch for airspeed, bank into the wind), recovery
- Rudder limit and how airspeed affects it
- Use on the approach; removing the slip at the round-out, sideslip for crosswind
- Limitations: airspeed indicator error in a slip, PA-28-161 slip/skid fuel caution, flaps per POH, stall characteristics in a slip vs skid
- Common errors

### 3. Schedule (ground about 35 min; flight about 40 min)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| What a slip is, forward slip vs sideslip | 10 min |
| Procedure, rudder limit, use on the approach | 15 min |
| Limitations and common errors | 10 min |
| Flight: demonstration at altitude, then on approach | 40 min |
| Postflight debrief | 10 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck (video)
- PA-28-161 POH (Section 2 and 4 notes on slips) and school checklist
- PA-28-161; model airplane

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Show forward slip vs sideslip with a model airplane and the AFH figures.
- Explain why indicated airspeed may be wrong in a slip and the PA-28-161 caution.
- Demonstrate at altitude first, then on a high approach.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain the difference between forward slip and sideslip.
- Enter, hold and recover from a forward slip while keeping the ground track and airspeed.
- Use a slip on a high approach and remove it before touchdown.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student uses a forward slip to correct a high approach while keeping the ground track aligned and airspeed within limits.
- The student recovers smoothly and lands with the longitudinal axis aligned with the runway.

### 8. References

- Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3), Ch. 9 Approaches and Landings (slips, forward slip to a landing, common errors)
- PA-28-161 Pilot's Operating Handbook: Sections 2 and 4 (slip notes)
- Private and Commercial Pilot Airman Certification Standards (forward slip to a landing)
