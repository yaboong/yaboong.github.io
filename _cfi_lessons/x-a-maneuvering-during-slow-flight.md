### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student can fly at an airspeed where any further increase in angle of attack, load factor or reduction in power would cause a stall warning, maintaining altitude, heading and coordination in straight, turning, climbing and descending flight, clean and in landing configuration.

### 2. Content

- Why slow flight; what it teaches (AFH objectives)
- Configuration and target speed (clean and landing; Vs1 51, Vs0 44 KIAS)
- How to fly slow and level: lift equation, angle of attack, induced drag, power required; pitch for airspeed, power for altitude
- Region of reversed command
- High angle of attack and high power effects: visibility, control effectiveness, [left-turning tendencies]({{ '/cfi-lesson-plan/module-left-turning-tendencies/' | relative_url }}), right rudder
- Turns in slow flight: load factor, stall speed increase, bank limits
- Weight and CG effects on stall speed (POH chart)
- Demonstrating flight characteristics: changing configuration (flaps) and airspeed, and showing the effect on control effectiveness, trim, pitch attitude and stall warning
- Exit procedure; private vs commercial standards
- Procedures (setup CRAAC; slowing down clean/landing configuration; level turn; climb; descent; recovery)
- AFH common errors; risks (collision hazards, distraction)

### 3. Schedule (ground about 1 hour; flight about 45 min)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Why, configuration, target speed | 10 min |
| Aerodynamics: AOA, drag, reversed command | 15 min |
| Control effects, turns, weight and CG | 15 min |
| Exit, standards, procedures | 15 min |
| Common errors and risks | 5 min |
| Flight: demonstration and practice | 45 min |
| Postflight debrief | 10 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck
- PA-28-161 POH (stall speeds, Vfe, flap limits) and school checklist
- PA-28-161

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Build up the aerodynamics step by step so the student understands why more power is needed at low airspeed.
- Brief the procedure numbers and standards; set the altitude floor.
- Demonstrate each configuration and maneuver, then coach right rudder, small bank and smooth power.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain the region of reversed command and why bank is limited in slow flight.
- Slow to the target airspeed in the briefed configuration without a stall warning.
- Fly level, turns, climbs and descents in slow flight and recover correctly.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student holds altitude ±100 ft (private; ±50 ft commercial), heading ±10°, airspeed +10/−0 kt, bank as specified, without activating the stall warning, finishing no lower than 1,500 ft AGL.
- The student explains how configuration and airspeed changes affect control effectiveness, trim and stall warning.

### 8. References

- Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3), Ch. 5 Maintaining Aircraft Control: Upset Prevention and Recovery Training (slow flight, common errors)
- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 5 Aerodynamics of Flight (lift equation, drag, region of reversed command, load factor)
- PA-28-161 Pilot's Operating Handbook: Section 2 (airspeed limitations), Section 5 (stall speeds)
- Private and Commercial Pilot Airman Certification Standards (maneuvering during slow flight)
