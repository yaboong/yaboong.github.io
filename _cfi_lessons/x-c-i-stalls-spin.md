### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student understands why stalls happen at the critical angle of attack, can recognize and recover from power-off, power-on, accelerated, cross-control, elevator trim and secondary stalls, and understands spin entry, spin aerodynamics and recovery.

### 2. Content

- Foundation: what a stall is, critical angle of attack, lift curve, factors affecting stall speed (weight, load factor, power, flaps, CG), POH stall speed chart, load factor in turns
- Recognition (impending vs full stall, senses, stall horn), AOA indicator, recovery steps, common errors, wing root stalls first, videos
- Stall types: table with minimum altitudes; power-off; power-on; accelerated; cross-control; base-to-final turn margins and how to avoid them; elevator trim stall; secondary stall
- Spins: before-spin checklist (PA-28-161 intentional spins prohibited), spin aerodynamics, phases, AFH spin figure, PARE recovery, turn indicators, spin vs spiral dive, videos

### 3. Schedule (ground about 1 hour 30 min; flight about 1 hour)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation: AOA, lift curve, stall speed factors | 20 min |
| Recognition and recovery | 15 min |
| Stall types and base-to-final risk | 25 min |
| Spins: aerodynamics, phases, recovery, spin vs spiral | 25 min |
| Review | 5 min |
| Flight: power-off, power-on, accelerated stalls (others demonstrated as appropriate) | 60 min |
| Postflight debrief | 10 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck (videos)
- PA-28-161 POH (stall speed chart, Section 2 spin prohibition, Section 3) and school checklist
- PA-28-161; model airplane

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Teach the stall as an angle-of-attack event and show how each factor changes stall speed.
- Brief the recovery steps and the minimum altitudes for each stall.
- Demonstrate each stall type with clear recognition cues; coach prompt, coordinated recovery.
- Teach spin awareness and the PARE recovery on the ground (intentional spins prohibited in the PA-28-161).

### 6. Student's Actions

- Explain critical angle of attack, the stall speed factors and load factor effects.
- Recognize impending and full stalls and recover with minimum altitude loss.
- Explain how each stall type happens in normal flight and how to avoid it.
- State the spin recovery and the difference between a spin and a spiral dive.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student recognizes stalls promptly and recovers by reducing angle of attack, leveling the wings, adding power and returning to the desired flight path, staying coordinated and above the minimum altitude.
- The student explains spin causes, phases and recovery correctly.

### 8. References

- Airplane Flying Handbook (FAA-H-8083-3), Ch. 5 Maintaining Aircraft Control: Upset Prevention and Recovery Training (stalls, stall types, spins, spin recovery)
- Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Ch. 5 Aerodynamics of Flight (angle of attack, stalls, load factor, spins)
- AC 61-67 (Stall and Spin Awareness Training)
- PA-28-161 Pilot's Operating Handbook: Section 2 (spins prohibited), Section 5 (stall speeds)
- Private and Commercial Pilot Airman Certification Standards (stalls, spin awareness); Flight Instructor standards for spins as applicable
