### 1. Lesson Objective

- The student can handle emergencies in the PA-28-161 using memory flows and the POH checklists: engine failure and emergency approach and landing, systems and equipment malfunctions, emergency descent, and the use of emergency equipment and survival gear.

### 2. Content

- Basics: forced vs precautionary landing vs ditching; psychological traps and startle; aviate-navigate-communicate; energy management; flow then checklist; when to declare
- Engine failure: A-B-C-D-E (best glide 73 KIAS, POH Section 3), best glide vs minimum sink, wind and density altitude effects; glide chart, ForeFlight glide ring
- Picking a field; declaring (MAYDAY vs PAN-PAN); power-off approach and shutdown items
- Engine failure after takeoff: straight ahead, the turnback, departure briefing; load factor in turns
- Common errors; memory flows for restart and shutdown
- Systems failures: partial power and rough engine, alternator failure (POH steps; YB's real alternator failure), pitot-static blockage and alternate static, vacuum vs all-electric panel, G5 failures, GPS loss
- Fires (electrical, engine start, in flight, cabin), door open, flap failures, elevator/trim failure, brake failure
- Emergency descent: why, setup, execution, recovery, speed limits, common errors
- Emergency equipment: ELT, fire extinguisher, survival gear, flotation, passenger briefing
- Key takeaways

### 3. Schedule (ground about 2 hours; flight about 1 hour)

| Segment | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Emergency basics and declaring | 15 min |
| Engine failure, glide, field selection, power-off approach | 30 min |
| Engine failure after takeoff, flows, errors | 15 min |
| Systems failures, fires, other malfunctions | 35 min |
| Emergency descent | 10 min |
| Emergency equipment and survival gear, review | 15 min |
| Flight: simulated engine failures, emergency descent, systems scenarios | 60 min |
| Postflight debrief | 10 min |

### 4. Equipment

- Laptop or projector with the deck (videos)
- PA-28-161 POH Section 3 Emergency Procedures and Section 5 glide chart; school emergency checklist
- PA-28-161 (G5, GNC 355, ELT, fire extinguisher); iPad with ForeFlight (glide ring)
- Survival kit example

### 5. Instructor's Actions

- Teach the memory flows first, then confirm each with the POH checklist.
- Use scenarios (including YB's real alternator failure) to practice decisions and communication.
- In flight, simulate failures safely (throttle to idle, never shutting down the engine) and coach field selection and the approach.

### 6. Student's Actions

- Recite and perform the A-B-C-D-E flow and the POH emergency checklists.
- Select a suitable field and fly a power-off approach to it.
- Identify and respond to systems failures and explain when to declare an emergency.
- Perform an emergency descent within limits and explain the emergency equipment.

### 7. Completion Standards

- The student establishes best glide promptly, selects a suitable field, completes the checklist and flies a safe approach (to a safe altitude set by the instructor).
- The student correctly analyzes and responds to each systems malfunction using the POH.
- The student performs an emergency descent within airspeed and configuration limits and explains the use of emergency equipment.
