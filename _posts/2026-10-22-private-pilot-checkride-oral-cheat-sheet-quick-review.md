## Qualification

- **student pilot** ([61.83](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.83), [61.87](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.87)): age 16 (airplane), read/speak/write/understand English
  - solo: pre-solo knowledge test + pre-solo flight training → solo endorsement (valid 90 days)
  - solo XC ([61.93](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.93)): solo XC endorsement + endorsement for each XC flight (planning reviewed by CFI)
  - class B ([61.95](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.95)): ground & flight training + endorsement within 90 days for that specific class B
- **student pilot limits** ([61.89](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.89)): no passengers, no compensation/hire, no furtherance of business, no international, vis↓[day 3SM / night 5SM], no flight without visual reference to surface, comply with endorsement limits
- **PPL eligibility** ([61.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.103)): age 17, English, at least student pilot cert, ground training endorsement → knowledge test, flight training endorsement → practical test
- **aeronautical experience (ASEL)** ([61.109](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.109)): total 40h (20h dual + 10h solo)
  - dual: 3h XC, 3h night (one XC 100NM↑ total & 10 TOL full stop), 3h instrument, 3h checkride prep within 2 cal months
  - solo: 5h solo XC, one solo XC 150NM↑ total (full stop at 3 points, one leg 50NM↑), 3 TOL full stop at towered airport
- **certificate**: PPL (issued without expiration date)
- **privilege & limit** ([61.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.113)): no PIC for compensation/hire, except
  - incidental to business/employment (no passengers/property for compensation/hire)
  - share operating expenses (fuel, oil, airport expenditures, rental fees) → pay at least pro rata share
  - charitable/nonprofit/community event flights (under 91.146)
  - search & location operations (reimbursement)
  - aircraft salesman (200h↑) demonstrating aircraft
- **pro rata share**: equal share per occupant (total cost ÷ number of occupants incl. pilot), e.g. $400 flight, pilot + 3 passengers → pilot pays ≥$100
- **required docs pilot** ([61.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.3)): PRIM (Pilot cert / Radio license(international) / ID-photo / Medical or BasicMed)
- **requirements to act as PIC**: PRIM, flight review, passenger currency (if carrying passengers), endorsements (if required), medical or BasicMed
- **endorsement** ([61.31](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.31)): complex, high-perf(200HP↑), pressurized(can fly FL250↑), tailwheel
- **complex**: retractable landing gear, flaps, controllable-pitch (constant-speed) props
- **type rating**: required for MTOW>12,500 lbs, turbojet-powered
- **logging flight time**: only required for [Cert/Rating/Flight-review/recent flight experience(61.57)]→CFR
- **flight review** ([61.56](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.56)): 24 cal months for PIC, 1hr flight & 1hr ground & endorsement
- **flight review exemption**: practical test (new cert/rating), FAA WINGS program
- **passenger currency** ([61.57](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.57)): 3 TOL 90days in same category/class (+type if required), full stop for night or tailwheel
- **night (log)**: [end of evening ~ beginning morning] civil twilight
- **night (currency)**: 1hr after sunset to 1hr before sunrise
- **night (lights)**: sunset to sunrise (position lights)
- **currency vs proficiency**: minimum legal requirement vs. ability to safely operate aircraft
- **unfamiliar aircraft/avionics**: [glass cockpit, new GPS, new airframe]→workload↑→task saturation→situational awareness↓→[deviation, loss of control, errors, airspace violations]
- **medical** ([61.23](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.23)): PPL → 3rd↑ or BasicMed, 3rd class [under 40: 60 cal months, 40↑: 24 cal months]
  - temporary disqualification ([61.53](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.53)): known medical deficiency or meds that make you unable to meet the medical standards → don't act as PIC (IMSAFE)
  - <details><summary><u>image: Under 40</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/01-qualification-02.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/01-qualification-02.jpg" alt="Under 40" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
  - <details><summary><u>image: Over 40</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/01-qualification-03.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/01-qualification-03.jpg" alt="Over 40" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
- **BasicMed**: alternative 3rd med, bypass AME→regular physician
  - requires: 48CMEC, 24online, treatment, US driver’s, FAA med cert after 7/14/2006
    - [CMEC (Comprehensive Medical Exam Checklist)](https://www.faa.gov/forms/index.cfm/go/document.information/documentID/1030366) ← [Updates](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/faa-updates-basicmed-program)
  - limit: US, max 7 occ. (6 passengers), MTOW 12500lbs↓, 18000MSL↓, 250kts↓, no compensation/hire
- **docs presentation**: [cert/med/log/+any] to administrator, NTSB, law enforcement officer
- **other rules**: address change 30days ([61.60](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.60)), DUI/DWI (motor vehicle action) report 60days ([61.15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-61.15))

## Regulation

- **required docs airplane**: ARROW (Airworthiness cert / Registration / Radio license(international) / Operating limitations(POH/AFM, placards, markings) / Weight & balance)
- **registration**: valid 7 years (since 2023), must be in the aircraft
- **airworthiness**: owner/operator responsible for maintaining (AVIATES), PIC is responsible for determining (preflight, maintenance log check)
- **airworthiness certificate**: FAA issued, proves aircraft conforms to its type design and is in a condition for safe operation, never expires (required inspections), displayed at cabin/cockpit entrance, standard(white)
- **special airworthiness certificate**: not qualify standard airworthiness, still capable of safe flight, pink paper, experimental/restricted/light-sport
- **required inspections**: AVIATES
  - **A**D: as required by each AD
  - **V**OR check: 30 days (IFR only, [91.171](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.171))
  - **I**nspection: annual 12 cal months, 100hr if for hire (can exceed 10hr to reach a place, deduct from next) ([91.409](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.409))
  - **A**ltimeter/static: 24 cal months (IFR only, [91.411](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.411))
  - **T**ransponder: 24 cal months ([91.413](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.413))
  - **E**LT: 12 cal months, battery replace [1hr cumulative use / 50% useful life] ([91.207](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.207))
  - **S**TC: as required
- **airworthiness directive (AD)**: FAA issued docs to correct an unsafe condition, mandatory to comply, owner/operator responsible, PIC verifies airworthiness
- **types of AD**: standard(within specific time), emergency(prior to further flight), one-time, recurring
- **supplemental type certificate (STC)**: FAA-approved major change to an existing Type Design. (e.g. sunvisor)
- **SAIB (Special Airworthiness Information Bulletins)**: FAA-issued, non-regulatory info/recommendation (doesn't meet AD criteria), not legally required, but highly recommended (manufacturer's version = Service Bulletin)
- **special flight permit**: aka ferry permit, not airworthy but safe to fly to repair location, via AWC tool or FSDO (FSDO will require A&P letter)
- **preventive maintenance** ([43.3(g)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-43.3)): pilot certificate holder, owned/operated aircraft(no part121/129/135), does not involve complex assembly operations
  - log→[description, date, name, signature, certificate number, kind of certificate]
  - e.g.→[[Appendix-A-to-Part-43(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/appendix-Appendix%20A%20to%20Part%2043#p-Appendix-A-to-Part-43(c)), landing gear tires, lights, bulbs, hydraulic fluid, ...]
- **maintenance type**: major, alteration, repair, preventive
- **preflight** ([91.103](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.103)): NWKRAFT (NOTAMs / Weather / Known ATC delays / Runway lengths / Alternates / Fuel / Takeoff & landing performance)
- **required equipments** ([91.205](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.205)): ATOMATOFLAMES(VFR-day), FLAPS(VFR-night), for [hire/water/↑glide]→device[flotation & pyrotechnic signal 1]
  - FLAPS: fuses/Landing light (only if for hire)/Anti-collision/Position lights/Source of electricity → landing light NOT required at night unless operated for hire ([91.205(c)(4)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/part-91/section-91.205#p-91.205(c)(4)))
- **inoperative** ([91.213(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/part-91/section-91.213#p-91.213(d))): no MEL?
  - not in [VFR-day type certification(POH/AFM)→KOEL(POH/AFM)→[91.205](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.205)→AD]?→ then ok
  - but, [remove/deactivate/placard "INOPERATIVE"] & record maintenance record, PIC determines no hazard
- **MEL**: minimum equipments requirement for safe flight with inoperative equipment (approved by FAA, LOA), with MEL → follow MEL
- **KOEL**: Kinds of Operation Equipment List (POH/AFM), which equipment required for [day/night/VFR/IFR]
- **ADS-B**: class[A/B/C], B→30NM, B/C→10000MSL↓, E→10000MSL↑(no 2500AGL↓)
- **mode-c xponder**: altitude reporting, class[A/B/C], 10000MSL↑(no 2500AGL↓)
- **minimum safe altitude** ([91.119](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.119)): anywhere[power fail→safe land], congested[↔2000,highest+1000↑], other-than-congested[500↑AGL], sparsely populated or water[no-limit but 500ft distance away from any person/property]
- **VFR cruising altitude** ([91.159](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.159)): 3000AGL↑, magnetic course 0~179(odd thousand+500), 180~359(even thousand+500)
- **fuel requirement** ([91.151](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.151)): (normal cruising speed) VFR-day[+30’], VFR-night[+45’]
- **safety belts** ([91.105](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.105), [91.107](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.107)): crew[cruise:belt,TOL:+harness], passenger[cruise:x,TOL:belt], exception[2yrs-old↓,parachuting], PIC briefing (how to fasten/unfasten)
- **alcohol & drug** ([91.17](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.17)): [4,8,16], 0.04<↓, no within 8hrs, 1oz→3hrs, can remain 16hrs, no intoxicated passengers (except emergency/medical care)
- **supplemental oxygen** ([91.211](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-91/subpart-C/section-91.211)): unpressurized?→12500<(min-crew,30min+)≤14000, 14000>min-crew, 15000>occupant provided
- **right-of-way** ([91.113](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.113)): distress > BGTS > engine, head-on→both turn right, converging→aircraft on the right, being overtaken has right-of-way (overtake on the right), landing→lower altitude (don't cut in)
- **speed limit** ([91.117](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.117)): 10000MSL↓→250kts↓, under class B or VFR corridor 200kts↓, within 4NM of class C/D primary & 2500AGL↓ → 200kts↓
- **SVFR** ([91.157](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.157)): below VFR [ceiling<1000, vis<3] but [CoC, vis≥1] unless NO-SVFR, surface area of controlled airspace below 10000MSL → ATC clearance, night → IR & IFR-equipped
- **aerobatic flight** ([91.303](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.303)): NO[congested-area/open-assembly/class B,C,D,E surface area/within 4NM of airway/1500AGL↓/vis<3SM]
- **parachute** ([91.307](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.307)): passengers + bank>60° or pitch>30° (nose up/down) → parachute required (except flight training/checkride with CFI)
- **dropping objects** ([91.15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-91.15)): allowed with reasonable precautions to avoid injury/damage
- **NTSB** ([830](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/part-830)): accident[death/serious injury/substantial damage] → notify immediately, report within 10 days (7 days if overdue aircraft), incident → immediate notification for listed items (e.g. flight control failure, in-flight fire, collision in flight)
- **PA28 class & category**: aircraft[category:normal/class:airplane], airmen[category:airplane/class:SEL]

## Systems

- PA-28-161: [FC LR HAND 160 2700], Four-cylinder, Carbureted, Lycoming engine (O-320), Reciprocating piston engine, Horizontally opposed, Air-cooled, Naturally aspirated, Direct-drive, 160HP at 2,700 RPM.
- **fuel system (POH) diagram**
  - <details><summary><u>image: fuel system (POH) diagram</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-01.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-01.jpg" alt="fuel system (POH) diagram" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
- **4-stroke**: intake(air/fuel mixture), compression, combustion(ignites→power), exhaust
- **engine components**: crankcase, cylinder, piston, connecting rod, crankshaft, valves, spark plug
  - <details><summary><u>image: engine components</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-02.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-02.jpg" alt="engine components" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
- **induction system**: brings air→mix with fuel→delivers mix to cylinder (4-stroke), best 14.7:1 (air:fuel), types[carburetor/fuel-injection]
- **carburetor**: types of induction system
  - <details><summary><u>image: carburetor</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-03.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-03.jpg" alt="carburetor" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
- **carburetor flow**: air inlet → air filter → carburetor venturi (static pressure↓→fuel comes in due to pressure diff) → mix(air/fuel) → cylinder → 4-stroke
- **carburetor icing**: -7~21℃(20~70°F)↓&humidity80%↑, venturi→temperature↓→ice→airflow↓→RPM↓
  - temperature↓ caused by: fuel vaporization, low power means cools↑ & venturi↑
- **carb heat**: heat exchanger(near exhaust manifold) provides hot air → anti-icing, but RPM↓(hot→density↓→air↓→rich→RPM↓)
- **fuel-injection**: fuel is injected directly into the cylinders
  - pros: easier cold weather start, fuel flow/distribution↑, throttle response↑, precise mixture control
  - cons: difficult hot weather start, vapor locks(fuel vaporize inside fuel line)
- **fuel-injection flow**: tank→aux fuel pump→engine-driven fuel pump→fuel-air control unit→fuel manifold valve→fuel lines with injectors
  - fuel pump: pumps fuel from fuel tank into the fuel-air control unit
  - fuel-air control unit: regulates the specific amount of fuel needed (based on the mixture/throttle settings), then the fuel gets sent to the fuel manifold valve
  - fuel manifold valve (distributer, a.k.a spider): split metered liquid fuel evenly to injection lines (splitting)
  - fuel lines (has fuel-injector): inject fuel to cylinder (spraying)
    - <details><summary><u>image: fuel lines (has fuel-injector)</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-04.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-04.jpg" alt="fuel lines (has fuel-injector)" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
- **throttle control**: controls air/fuel mixture, rapid increase→detuning engine crankshaft counterweights
- **mixture control**: controls air/fuel ratio, altitude↑→air density↓, but fuel density same→lean (3000ft↑), too lean→engine temp↑, too rich→spark plug fouling→power↓
- **ignition systems**: independent of electrical system (but, requires battery to start)
  - ignition switch on: disconnect p-lead→battery runs starter motor→crank shaft rotate→magneto rotate→generate electricity→spark plug generates spark→combustion
  - ignition switch off: grounded by ground wire(p-lead)→block electricity →no electricity no combustion
  - each cylinder → 2 spark plug (redundancy&better ignition) ignite air/fuel mixture
  - each spark plug → connected to different(L/R) magneto (redundancy)
  - magneto: permanent magnet, engine-driven (crankshaft) → generates its own current, ignition switch only grounds(off)/ungrounds
    - <details><summary><u>image: magneto</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-05.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-05.jpg" alt="magneto" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
- **engine abnormalities**: engine temperature↑→detonation/pre-ignition→power↓
  - reduce power, rich mixture, lower nose (air cool)
- **detonation**: uncontrolled explosive ignition, caused by[incorrect fuel grade, too lean, too long ground ops in hot weather, high MP with low RPM]
- **pre-ignition**: ignites before normal ignition, caused by hot spots [carbon deposits, overheated spark plugs, cracked ceramic insulator]
- **cold weather**: pre-heat thickened oil
- **oil system**: LCCCseals lubricates, cools, cleans, corrosion↓, seals
- **oil system components**: sump, filter, pump, gauges
- **oil extreme caution**: [oil-pressure↓,oil-temp↑]→land ASAPossible
  - vicious cycle[oil pressure↓ ↔ oil temp↑] → engine damage & fail
  - oil temp↑→viscosity↓→leak→oil pressure↓→friction↑→heat↑→oil temp↑
  - oil temp↑←[too lean, too much power, low oil, clogged cooler]
  - oil pressure↓←[low oil, pump fail, weary bearing]
- **engine cooling**: engine temp[EGT(for lean), CHT], cool [oil/air/liquid], too long high pitch climb→engine temp↑→cruise climb
- **exhaust system**: burnt air/fuel mixture→exhaust, utilize heat[cabin/carb/defrost], carbon monoxide poisoning
- **propeller**: rotating airfoil, creates thrust, direct drive (directly connected to crankshaft), thrust←[airfoil shape, blade AoA, speed]
- **blade twist**: blade twist for uniform thrust, hub[speed↓ blade angle↑], tip[speed↑ blade angle↓] → AoA ~constant along the blade
- **fixed pitch propeller**: fixed angle, optimized (climb/cruise)
  - climb: blade angle↓→AoA↓,pitch↓→smaller bite→spin fast→RPM↑,takeoff roll↓
  - cruise: blade angle↑→AoA↑,pitch↑→bigger bite→spin slow→RPM↓,takeoff roll↑
  - best efficiency?→only at a specific airspeed & RPM
- **propeller blade angle**
  - <details><summary><u>image: propeller blade angle</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-06.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/03-systems-06.jpg" alt="propeller blade angle" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
- **prop AoA**: angle [chord line↔RW], not in motion → RW==0 → “blade angle==prop AoA”
  - prop AoA = blade angle - RW angle
- **optimal prop AoA**: 2°~4° → depends on aircraft speed
  - faster→forward velocity↑→RW angle↑(more parallel to prop)→prop AoA↓
  - slower→forward velocity↓→RW angle↓(less parallel to prop)→prop AoA↑
  - optimization → adjustable blade angle → “constant-speed prop” → max efficiency
- **complex aircraft (FYI, not on PA-28-161)**: constant-speed propeller (blue lever→RPM, governor keeps RPM by changing blade angle, MP gauge→power), retractable landing gear (gear lights 3 green, gear warning horn, emergency gear extension)
- **landing gear (PA-28-161)**: fixed tricycle gear, nose wheel steering via rudder pedals, hydraulic disc brakes (toe brakes)
- **vacuum system**: engine-driven vacuum pump → attitude & heading indicators, check suction gauge, vacuum fail → partial panel (turn coordinator is electric)
- **electrical system (PA-28-161)**
  - battery (12V, 25Amp-hour): provides power to starter, backup for alternator fail (typically 30min)
  - alternator (14V, 60Amp): primary power source, engine-driven, connected to crankshaft, charge battery
  - ammeter: measures current flow to(charging+) or from(discharding-), (-) means alternator failure (system’s draining battery)
  - loadmeter: no (-) sign, shows total load supplied by alternator, electric load↑→indicate↑, 0→alternator failure
  - fuses/circuit breaker: prevents overloading, fuses→break→replace, circuit breaker→pops→fix&reset
  - electrical consumers: electric fuel pump, ignition switch(starter), pitot heat, comm/nav, transponder, ADS-B, GPS, audio panel, G5, lights [landing/position(nav)/beacon/strobe/instrument-panel]
- **environmental systems**: maintain "cabin altitude" much lower than the actual flight altitude (typically 8,000ft or less)
  - alt.↑→pressure↓→air density↓→requires[pressurized/oxygen↑]
  - pressurized: airliner uses bleed air from compressor in engine, largeGA uses turbocharger/supercharger from engine → maintain inside pressure↑
  - depressurization(decompression): when pressure system fails
    - explosive de.: pressure change (<0.5s) is faster than lung’s equalization → potential lung damage
    - rapid de.: pressure change (>0.5s) is slower than lung’s equalization, no lung damage but hypoxia↑
  - oxygen system: oxygen↓→hypoxia, FAA recommends oxygen [10000ft day, 5000ft night]
    - ignition source(oil/grease/even lip balm..)+oxygen → fire
    - supplemental oxygen (91.211)
- **heat systems**: outside air flows through shroud(surrounds exhaust muffler→hot), crack in muffler?→deadly carbon monoxide poisoning (colorless, odorless)
- **anti-icing/deicing**: anti-icing(prevention), deicing(removal), use several times
  - for airfoil: inflatable deicing boots, thermal anti-ice leading edge, weeping wings
  - for windshield: alcohol anti-ice, electric heating anti-ice, defroster
  - for propeller: alcohol anti-ice, electric heating anti-ice, deicing boots
  - others: pitot heat, carb heat
  - residual ice↑ with [airspeed↓/temperature↓]
  - PA28: no de-icing, anti-icing (carbheat/pitot-heat/defroster)
- **aircraft lights**: landing, beacon(shows engine running, flashing red on tail), strobe(anti-collision, sparky), position/navigation (red:L, green:R, white:back)
- **ELT**: sends signal (in-impact/manual), emergency freq (121.5/243.0military), inspect 12 cal months, battery replace[1hr-usage/50%-life]
- **FADEC**: computer controls engine&props, no [magneto, carb heat, mixture, engine prime, prop pitch]→only throttle control, backup electric system required

## Flight Instruments

- **pitot-static**: pitot tube (dynamic p), static port (ambient static p), pitot mast
- **altimeter**: measure altitude above specific ref (sea level, Kollsman window), after set→within 75ft airport elev, high to low → look out below, 18000↑→29.92→FL180
  - altitude↑→pressure↓→aneroid wafer expands→mechanical linkage→indicate
  - kollsman window↑→indicated altitude↑
- **VSI**: shows rate-of-climb in fpm, trend→immediate, rate→6-9sec delay after established
  - diaphragm directly connected static source, chamber receives static p via calibrated leak, climb/descend→diaphragm p change instantly, chamber change slowly→pressure diff→mechanical linkage→shows rate of climb
- **ASI**: measures diff between impact(ram) air p (pitot tube) and ambient p (static port)
  - how: dynamic p (airspeed)=impact p - static p, diaphragm receives ram air p from pitot tube [faster→expand], air p↓ as alt↑ → ASI indicate slower → to correct → apply ambient p from static port → shows dynamic p as airspeed
- **pitot-static blockage**
  - static port: airspeed (alt↑→speed↓, correct only at blocked), altimeter(completely blocked→freeze), VSI(calibrated leak→equalize→0)
  - using alternate static source → venturi effect → lower pressure
    - airspeed↑, altimeter↑, VSI (momentarily climb)
  - pitot tube: only ASI, use pitot-heat
    - ram air inlet (x) & drain hole (o) → as if it’s parked → ASI 0 (zero)

- **4-force**: lift==weight→straight&level, thrust==drag→unaccelerated
  - lift: by wing(airfoil), applied at CP/CL(center of pressure/lift), perpendicular to [relative wind, flight path], counteract weight
  - weight: result of gravity, where CG applied
  - thrust: by propeller(airfoil)→generate horizontal lift
  - drag: opposite of thrust, parallel to [relative wind, flight path]
- **lift principle**:
  - Newton’s 3rd law: every action→equal/opposite reaction, airfoil shape and AoA accelerate the airflow downward, wing pushes air down→air pushes wing up→lift
  - Bernoulli’s principle: faster air→pressure↓, wing shape→faster air on top→lower pressure, while slow air underneath maintains high pressure, pressure diff→lift
- **lift formula**: `L = ½ρV²SC_L`  ⇒ (simplified) `L = V × a`(AoA)
  - `ρ (rho)`: air density ← [altitude/temperature/humidity]↑→air↓→for same lift?→TAS↑
  - `V²`: velocity
  - `C_L`: coefficient of lift ← AoA (`C_L`max == critical AoA)
  - `S`: wing area ← flap
- **parasite drag**: byproduct of flying through the air, increases with **speed**, airspeed↑→parasite drag↑
  - proportional to the square of the airspeed: airspeed*1/2→p-drag*1/4, airspeed*2→p-drag*4
  - form drag: by shape, anything sticks out, antenna, landing gear
  - interference drag: by intersection airstreams, this(wing)drag+that(fuselage)drag⇒even more drag(1+1>2)→fairings

  - skin friction drag: by rough/dirty surface (dirt, rivets)
- **(lift) induced drag**: “byproduct of lift, decreases with **speed**”
  - `L = qSC_L` (`q = ½ρV²` = dynamic pressure)
  - airspeed(V)↑→q↑→requires ↓AoA to maintain level→wingtip vortex↓→induced drag↓
  - airspeed(V)↓→q↓→requires ↑AoA to maintain level→wingtip vortex↑→induced drag↑

## Performance & W/B

- **types of altitude**: indicated(from altimeter), true altitude (MSL), absolute altitude (AGL), pressure altitude, density altitude
- **pressure altitude**: corrected for non-standard pressures
  - PA = [(29.92 - Current Pressure) * 1000] + Airport Elevation
- **density altitude**: pressure altitude corrected for non-standard temperatures
  - DA = [(Local Temperature) - (Standard Temperature)] * 120 + PA

  - CG (center of gravity): point where weight is applied (balance point), must remain within limit(=envelope), “Total Moment ÷ Total Weight”
  - CP (center of pressure=CL): point where lift is applied (informal: "center of lift"), CG normally forward of CP
  - CG range: distance between the CG limits
  - datum: imaginary vertical line from which all arms are measured
    - → reference point to measure arm
  - arm: distance from the datum to where the weight (force) is applied
    - → pilot and front seat: 80.5 → distance from the datum is 80.5 inch
  - station: location of an object in relationship to the datum (similar to the arm)
  - moment: weight * arm (distance from the reference point)
  - SEW (standard empty weight): airplane + unusable fuel(2lbs) + operating fluids(oil)
  - BEW (basic empty weight): SEW + optional equipment
  - MGW (max gross weight): BEW + max useful load
  - useful load: piot + passengers + cargo + usable fuel (MTOW-BEW)
  - MTOW (max takeoff weight): max weight for safe takeoff
  - MRW (max ramp weight): max weight on ground (including fuel before start-up←can be higher than MTOW←fuel burn during taxi)
  - ZFW (zero fuel weight): BEW + payload(passengers+cargo)
    - ZFW limit: structural limit to prevent wing root stress in flight
    - Not for PA-28-161
- **taildown force & drag**: GA aircraft → CL(center of lift) is located aft of CG
  - lift generated → nose-down moment → counteract? → taildown force↑ → nose-up
    - <details><summary><u>image: lift generated → nose-down moment → counteract? → taildown f</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/06-performance-w-b-02.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/06-performance-w-b-02.jpg" alt="lift generated → nose-down moment → counteract? → taildown f" loading="lazy"/></a><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/06-performance-w-b-03.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/06-performance-w-b-03.jpg" alt="lift generated → nose-down moment → counteract? → taildown f" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
  - total lift (at CL) = aircraft weight + taildown force
  - taildown force↑→lift↑→induced drag↑ (vice versa)
- **CG**: always located forward of the CL (both aft CG and forward CG)
- **aft CG**: CG/CL closer→nose-up→↓taildown force→wing load↓→stall speed↓
- (critical AoA is reached at a lower airspeed due to decreased wing loading)
  - ↓taildown force→req lift↓→AoA↓→induced drag↓
  - pros) stall speed↓,cruise speed↑,fuel efficiency↑,range↑,takeoff roll↓
  - cons) stability↓,control efficiency↓(harder stall recovery),over rotation/flare on TO/L
  - key) ↓stall speed, but harder to recover
- **forward CG**: CG/CL farther→nose-down→↑taildown force→wing load↑→stall speed↑ (critical AoA is reached at a higher airspeed due to increased wing loading)
  - ↑taildown force→req lift↑→AoA↑→induced drag↑
  - pros) stability↑,control efficiency↑(easier stall recovery)
  - cons) stall speed↑,cruise speed↓,fuel efficiency↓,range↓,takeoff roll↑,flare difficult
  - key) ↑stall speed, but easier to recover

- **why these cloud clearances?**
  - B → CoC: heavy traffic, wider cloud clearance would only limit ATC's flexibility → relaxed weather rule, strict ATC rule
  - G 1200↓ day → 1 CoC: low & slow traffic, no ATC/radio required, stricter minimums would make flying almost impossible → "see and avoid clouds carefully"
  - C/D/E 10000↓ → 3152: controlled airspace, VFR & IFR mixed → room to see IFR traffic popping out of clouds
  - E/G 10000↑ → 5111: no 250kt limit above 10000 → need to see farther

## Navigation & XC Planning

- [**YB NAVLOG**](https://yaboong.github.io/navlog/): VFR navigation log generator
- **my posts**
  - [Direct Isn't Always Best: VFR Pilotage Route Planning]({% post_url 2026-08-16-VFR-pilotage-route-planning-cross-country-nav-log %})
  - [Reinventing the Wheel: Why I Built a New VFR Navigation Log Format]({% post_url 2026-08-17-why-YB-NAVLOG %})
- **pilotage**: navigation by visual reference to landmarks (checkpoints) on the sectional chart
- **dead reckoning**: navigation by computing heading/groundspeed/time from known TAS, wind, and course
- **route planning**: airspace (B/C/D, SUA, TFR), terrain & obstacles (MEF), checkpoints every ~10NM, alternates along the route, glide distance
- **altitude selection**: VFR cruising altitude (3000AGL↑), terrain/obstacle clearance, winds aloft, glide distance, fuel burn, cloud clearance
- **course & heading**: TC → (±WCA) → TH → (±variation, east least west best) → MH → (±deviation) → CH
- **wind correction**: E6B or EFB, wind from right→correct right, headwind→GS↓
- **time, speed, distance**: time = distance / GS, fuel = time × fuel burn, ETA in local & UTC
- **VFR flight plan**: file with FSS (1800wxbrief / 1-800-WX-BRIEF), activate after departure (FSS), close at destination, not closed → SAR begins 30 min after ETA
  - items: aircraft ID, type/equipment, TAS, departure point & time, altitude, route, destination, ETE, fuel on board (hours), alternate, pilot info, number aboard, color
- **flight following**: VFR radar advisories (traffic), workload permitting, ask approach/center, squawk assigned code
- **transponder codes**: 1200 (VFR), 7500 (hijack), 7600 (lost comm), 7700 (emergency)
- **lost communication (VFR)**: squawk 7600, light gun signals from tower, remain VFR, land as soon as practicable
- **diversion**: decide early, turn toward the alternate first, estimate heading/distance/time/fuel (rule of thumb), then refine, tell ATC/FSS, update flight plan
- **lost procedures** (5C): climb, communicate (121.5 or ATC), confess, comply, conserve (fuel)
- **interception** ([AIM 5-6-13](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap5_section_6.html)): follow interceptor's instructions, rock wings to acknowledge, squawk 7700 (unless told otherwise), try 121.5 / 243.0, don't make sudden maneuvers

#### a. Atmospheric composition and stability

- **atmosphere**: [nitrogen78/oxygen21/argon0.9/water-vapor0-4]%, troposphere[0-10km/weather/convective-activity/alt↑→temp↓], stratosphere[10-50km/ozone/airlines/alt↑→temp↑], tropopause

- **tropopause**: about 36000ft but level varies (↓at poles,↑ at equator), abrupt change in temperature & lapse rate, below tropopause?→standard temperature/pressue lapse rate remains the same(2℃↓/1”Hg per 1000ft)
- **stability**: determined by [moisture/temperature/lapse-rate], warm/humid→unstable, cold/dry→stable, heating→stability↓, cooling→stability↑
- **stable air**: resists vertical motion, stratiform, smooth air, steady precipitation, fair-poor vis
- **unstable air**: promotes vertical motion, towering(cumuliform), rough air, showery precipitation, t-storms., good vis

#### b. Wind (e.g., windshear, mountain wave, factors affecting wind, etc.)

- **wind**: change in pressure→horizontal movement of air→wind
- **isobar**: millibars, connect points equal pressure, closer→pressure gradient↑→wind↑
- **factors affecting wind**: pressure diff [diff↑→wind↑]
  - coriolis force: earth’s rotation(counter clock-wise, left-to-right, west-to-east), deflect wind/air right Northern Hem., poles↑equator:0
    - <details><summary><u>image: coriolis force</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-05.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-05.jpg" alt="coriolis force" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
  - friction btwn air/surface: slows down wind→coriolis force↓→wind flow across isobars at slight angle toward low pressure (surface wind slower & turb. than winds aloft)
  - gravity: vertical air movements (downbursts/microbursts)
- **circulation**: day[hot land→pressure↓ & cool sea→pressure↑] → wind[sea→land], night[cool land→pressure↑ & warm sea→pressure↓] → wind[land→sea]
- **temp inversion**: temp↑ as alt↑, hot-day&cool-night→ ground cools → still hot above → warm air lid → trapping cooler air → vis↓(smog/haze)&low-level wind shear
- **wind shear**: sudden change wind speed/direction in small area
  - sudden drastic change in IAS, affect lift & control, low-level WS (less time to recover)
  - mainly occurs: t-storms, frontal boundary (cold front), temperature inversion, CAT at high, microbursts
- **mountain wave**: when 15kts↑ & 30° to ridge, upwind(smooth), but crest-to-downwind?→↑downdraft(hundreds miles)→severe turb, lenticular/rotor cl, approach ridge?→45°(to avoid in case of severe downdraft without ↑load-factor)

#### c. Temperature and heat exchange

- **standard day**: 15℃ (59℉), 29.92”Hg (1013.2hPa or mb)
- **heat exchange**: uneven heating→ warm(less dense)↑ & cold(denser)↓ → changes[pressure/temperature/+friction] → air movement → wind&convective current
- **adiabatic**: temperature change due to rise/sink (without heat exchange with outside)
  - cooling: air rise→surrounding pressure↓→expands→internal energy used→temp.↓
  - heating: air sink→surrounding pressure↑→compress→energy added parcel→temp.↑
- **lapse rate**: rate at which temp↓ as alt↑, standard 1000’↑ [2℃/1”Hg]↓,
- steeper(temp↓rapidly as alt↑) → unstable/convective act
- **sublimation**: solid→gas (without liquid phase), add/remove moisture to air
- **evaporation**: liquid→vapor, adds moisture
- **condensation**: vapor→liquid, removes moisture
- **vertical motion**: caused by [orographic, convective(buoyancy), frontal] → clouds

#### d. Moisture/precipitation

- **relative humidity**: `(water in air mass / total water capacity) × 100%`, depends on temperature
  - warm air→hold more water
  - temperature↓→relative humidity↑→condensation↑→more visible moisture
  - temperature↑→relative humidity↓→condensation↓→less visible moisture
- **dew point**: temperature at which air becomes saturated → no longer hold water vapor → condensation begins (water vapor → liquid)
  - closer temp&dewpoint→humidity↑visible moisture↑(fog/clouds/dew/frost/rain)→ vis↓
  - added moisture → dew point↑ (means, condensation can begin at a higher temperature == less cool down is required for condensation)
  - dew point below freezing & aircraft surface at or below freezing → frost
  - visible moisture + temp ≤0℃ → icing (air temp can't go below dew point)
- **precipitation**: form of water that falls from the atmosphere (rain/drizzle/sleet/virga/hail)
  - sleet: snow(solid, ice crystals)+freezing rain(rapid structural icing)
  - hail: ice pellets produced by strong updrafts in thunderstorms
- **freezing rain & ice pellet**: rain→ice (rain above, freezing at current altitude → colder below → inversion)
  - <details><summary><u>image: freezing rain & ice pellet</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-06.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-06.jpg" alt="freezing rain & ice pellet" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
  - temperature inversion: above warm layer → cold → snow → [snow→rain] at warm layer → [rain→ice] at cold surface → ice pellets at surface means [freezing rain/supercooled water] at higher altitude
  - rain from air 0℃↑ → into air 0℃↓
  - rain freezes on impact
  - temp could be 0℃↑ at higher alt.
  - freezing rain→warm air above, freezing drizzle→often NO warm layer (collision-coalescence)
- **wet snow**: ice→rain (snow above, melting at current altitude), wet snow at your alt.→ temp>freezing → freezing level above
  - <details><summary><u>image: wet snow</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-07.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-07.jpg" alt="wet snow" loading="lazy"/></a></details>

#### e. Weather system formation, including air masses and fronts

- **low pressure**: air moves[inward/upward/counter-clockwise], generally cloudy&unstable, often rain/snow, rising air cools→moisture condense→[form clouds,icing risk]
- **high pressure**: air moves[outward/downward/clockwise], typically clear&stable, sinking air warms up→preventing clouds forming
- **adiabatic compression**: high pressure → air mass sinks → surrounding pressure↑ while sinking → air parcel compress → compression generates heat → temperature↑ → hold more water → relative humidity↓ → less visible moisture
- **trough**: brown, elongated area of low p., air↑(can’t sink or spread)→cloud forms&precipitation→often bad weather, can bring bad weather even inside high pressure
- **ridge**: yellow, elongated area of high p., air↓→often good weather
- **fronts**: boundaries between two air masses (cold front doesn’t necessarily mean that it’s colder than warm front, relative concepts)
  - flying across a front? → wind direction & temperature change
  - squall line? → (50~200 mile) ahead of a cold front, sudden wind 16~22kts↑
  - cold front moves over mountainous? → icing
  - frontal waves? → slowly moving cold fronts or stationary fronts
  - low level wind shear? → warm front (temperature inversion)
- **cold front (blue)**: [cold/dense/fast-moving/20-30kts or faster] air forces warm air to rise rapidly → steep frontal surface, generally more intense
  - hazards: rapid weather change(few hours), towering cumulous(nimbus), strong turbulence, gusty winds, heavy precip, squall line t-storms., hail, tornadoes
  - after passage: temperature↓, pressure↑, clearing skies, visibility↑, wind shifts clock wise (because the COLD front brings in relatively denser air with high pressure)
  - cold front
    - <details><summary><u>image: cold front</u></summary><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-08.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-08.jpg" alt="cold front" loading="lazy"/></a><a href="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-09.jpg" target="_blank"><img src="/posts_image/2026-10-21/cpl/11-weather-information-k3-meteorology-09.jpg" alt="cold front" loading="lazy"/></a></details>
- **warm front (red)**: [warm/less-dense/slow-moving/10-15kts] air glides up over cooler air mass → gradual frontal slope, temperature inversion, generally less intense, produce [wide area of weather/stratiform/ceiling↓/vis↓/rain/drizzle&fog], but summer?→cumulous&thunderstorm possible
  - hazards: precipitation fog, freezing rain→icing (warm rain falls into cold air layer and freeze)

#### g. Turbulence

- **turbulence**: unsteady/irregular motion of air
  - thermal turb: hot days→uneven heating→warm air↑→cumulus clouds & storms
  - mechanical turb: wind blowing over [rough gound/mountains]→choppy air
  - frontal turb: two air masses(cold front)→warm air↑→instability&bumps
  - CAT: invisible in clear sky, often near jet stream (due to strong wind shear)
- **turbulence intensity**
  - light: momentarily changes in [altitude/attitude(+yaw)]
  - mod: changes in [IAS/altitude/attitude(+yaw)], but aircraft remains positive control at all times
  - severe: large/abrupt changes in [IAS/altitude/attitude(+yaw)], aircraft momentarily out of control
- **CAT** (clear air turbulence): sudden severe turbulence, cloudless region, violent buffeting, strong WS(60-90kts), typically [found:above 15000AGL(20000-50000’ cloud-free area) or tropopause, caused by: mountain-wave/jet-stream], curved jet-stream /w deep low pressure trough → greatest turbulence, possibly identified through long cirrus clouds, upper trough on polar side of jet stream
- **jet stream**: wind>50kts, can go up to 240kts, 4-8miles (20000-40000’) in height (near tropopause), winter→temp.diff between polar&equator↑→stronger, [winter/stronger/south]↔[summer/weaker/north]

#### i. Icing and freezing level information

- **PA28**: defroster, carb heat, pitot heat
- **intensity**: trace, light, moderate(accumulation 1~3inches/hour), severe
- **low-pressure areas**: rising air → cool down → visible moisture↑ → icing risk↑
- **performance degradation:** 1/2 inch ice → lift↓ by 50%
  - icing→disrupts airflow(lift↓thrust↓drag↑weight↑)→AoA↑ for lift→ stall speed↑
  - approach speed↑, landing distance↑
- **mitigate**: avoid visible moisture at/below 0℃, encountered?→exit immediately (180° turn, climb/descend to warmer air), pitot heat/carb heat/defroster ON
  - hand fly, find cloud base/top, leave visible moisture (climb/descend/turn), warmer temperature, lower humidity
- **types**: instrument, induction, structural
  - instrument icing: forms on instruments & sensors → inaccurate readings → pitot-heat
  - induction icing: forms in engine’s air intake system → airflow↓ → disrupts air/fuel mixture → engine performance↓ → engine failure (carb icing)
    - carburetor icing: forms when [temperatures (-7~21℃, 70℉) / relative humidity above 80%], due to steep temperature drop in carburetor venturi → carb heat
  - structural icing: forms when visible moisture contacts below freezing aircraft surface, forms on exterior surface (wings/tail/props) → change airfoil shape → risk↑
    - clear: large droplet spreads. [smooth/clear/hard/dense]→difficult [detect/remove]
    - rime: small droplets freeze instantly. [milky(rough&white) color, brittle texture] → easier to spot, forms on thinner parts (wing edges)
    - mixed: when [snow/rain] coexists(unstable/mixed clouds), severe perf loss
- **+structural icing**: “most likely”→freezing rain (“least” likely→high clouds)
- **freezing level**: lowest altitude temperature 0°C, know it before flight, sources [AWC/winds aloft forecast (FB)/PIREP/weather briefing]
- **known icing**: PA-28 not certified for flight into known icing → avoid, never take off with [frost/ice/snow] on the airplane
- **icing on airfoils**: shape change→weight↑lift↓(less noticeable at low AoA)→stall speed↑
- **plan&predict** (CAFFU): Cloud layers, Air temperature & pressure (more likely in low-pressure areas, cool temperatures), Fronts movement, Freezing level, Use weather briefing (METAR/TAF/AIRMET/SIGMET)
- **anti-icing/deicing**: anti-icing(prevention), deicing(removal)
  - for airfoil: inflatable deicing boots, thermal anti-ice leading edge, weeping wings
  - for windshield: alcohol anti-ice, electric heating anti-ice, defroster
  - for propeller: alcohol anti-ice, electric heating anti-ice, deicing boots
  - others: pitot heat, carb heat

#### j. Fog/mist

- **fog** (PAIRUS): cloud near surface(50’), air cools to its dew point & moisture added
  - Precipitation: warm rain falls through nearly saturated cold air → moisture evaporates → humidity↑ → [added moisture→dewpoint↑] → air saturated(condensation) → fog. happens [warm front / slow-moving cold front / temperature inversion]
  - Advection: warm/moist air moves over cooler surface (coastal areas), opposite of steam fog, forms quickly, cover large areas, wind<15kts (15kts↑?→low stratus cloud)
  - Ice: extremely cold (below -25°F), Arctic regions
  - Radiation: calm/clear night(valley), ground cools air above, dissipate with sunrise or light wind
  - Upslope: wind pushes moist/stable air to upsloping terrain (mountain obscuration)
  - Steam: cold/dry air moves over warm water, looks like steam’s rising from the water, common in winter (/w low-level WS & icing)
- **fog(FG) vs mist(BR)**: fog(vis<5/8mile), mist(5/8≤vis<7) is often dissipating form of fog

#### k. Frost

- surface below freezing, water vapor condenses onto that surface
- effects: even thin layer→significantly disrupt airflow→lift↓drag↑→stall speed↑
- hazards: dangerous especially during takeoff, block [pitot tubes, static port]
- removal: [anti-icing/de-icing] fluid, wipe off, melt in [heated hanger, sun]

#### l. Obstructions to visibility (e.g., smoke, haze, volcanic ash, etc.)

- **Haze (HZ)**: suspended(floating) fine/dry particles, causes visual illusions→makes runway/terain farther→low approach
- **Smoke (FU)**:widespread low visibility, temperature inversion→worst
- **Volcanic Ash (VA)**: extereme danger, sandblasting→[ruins windshield(scratch), blocks pitot-tubes, melts inside jet turbines(engine failure)], SIGMETs.

## Night Operations

- **night vision**: rods(night & peripheral vision), cones(color & central vision), night blind spot at center → off-center viewing (5~10° away), scan slowly
- **dark adaptation**: about 30 min, avoid bright white light (dim red light can impair map colors), oxygen↓ at altitude → night vision↓ (FAA recommends oxygen 5000ft↑ at night)
- **night illusions**: black-hole approach (dark terrain → feels high → low approach), autokinesis (staring at a single light), false horizon (city lights/stars), featureless terrain
- **position lights**: red(left wing), green(right wing), white(tail)
  - red & green → coming toward you (head-on)
  - only red → crossing right to left, only green → crossing left to right
  - only white → moving away from you
- **night equipment & lights**: FLAPS (91.205(c)), position lights sunset to sunrise (91.209), anti-collision lights (if equipped)
- **personal equipment**: flashlight (white & red, spare batteries), charts with backup, know switch locations by feel
- **airport lighting**: beacon (white/green civil), PCL (7/5/3 clicks in 5 sec), runway edge (white, instrument rwy → yellow last 2000ft or half rwy, whichever less), taxiway edge (blue), obstruction lights (red/white)

## Number & Formula

- **descend plan**: 3° glide path → GS*5(or GS/2*10) fpm with 1000ft per 3NM
  - distance(NM) = (altitude to lose / 1000) × 3 (or altitude to lose / 300)
    - e.g. descend 2500ft → 2.5 × 3 = 7.5NM + safety margin(3NM)
  - speed(fpm): GS/2*10 or (GS*5)
    - e.g. GS 100 → 100 × 5 = 500fpm, GS 120 → 120 / 2 × 10 = 600fpm
- Unit
  - 1SM = 5280ft
- PA (pressure altitude) = (Standard Pressure − Current Pressure) * 1000 + Indicated Altitude
- Pressure Altitude (PA)
  - PA = Altitude (field elevation) + (29.92 - current altimeter setting) x 1000
  - in flight → altitude shown with 29.92 set
- Density Altitude (DA)
  - DA = PA + [120 x (outside air temp.in degrees Celsius – standard temp.**for that altitude**)]
- ft/NM, ft/min
  - ft/NM given → required ft/min (what to set on the VSI)
    - **(ft per min)** = GS / 60 * (ft per NM)
  - ft/min (VSI) & GS given → ft/NM
    - **(ft per NM)** = (ft per min) * 60 / GS
